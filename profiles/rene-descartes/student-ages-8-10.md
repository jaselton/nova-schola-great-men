# René Descartes: Student Lesson (Ages 8-10)
## The Man Who Wanted Clear Thinking

---

## The Essential Question

**How can we think clearly when a problem is confusing?**

---

## The Story

René Descartes was born in France in 1596. He went to a very good school and learned many subjects: languages, stories, philosophy, and mathematics.

But Descartes noticed something. People often disagreed, even when they sounded very confident. Some answers were repeated just because they were old. Descartes wanted to know:

> What can I know clearly?

He loved mathematics because math moved step by step. If you made each step carefully, you could solve hard problems.

---

## Descartes's Method

Descartes believed the mind needs a method. A method is a careful way of doing something.

His method can be made simple for students:

1. Do not rush.
2. Ask what you know clearly.
3. Break the hard problem into smaller parts.
4. Work in order.
5. Check your answer.

This is useful for math, reading, chores, arguments, and almost any hard task.

---

## “I Think, Therefore I Am”

Descartes became famous for saying:

> I think, therefore I am.

That sounds strange at first. He meant something careful: if I am thinking, wondering, or asking questions, then I must exist. A thought cannot happen without someone thinking it.

Descartes was looking for one thing he could know for certain.

---

## The Warning

Doubt can help us test an answer. But doubt can also become a bad habit.

If you doubt everything all the time, you may stop learning. Descartes did not want to stay confused. He used doubt to find truth.

So the lesson is:

**Ask careful questions. Do not live in endless doubt.**

---

## The Lesson

Descartes teaches us:

> Use your mind well.

A smart person should not rush, guess, or show off. A smart person should think clearly, work step by step, and check the answer.

---

## Practice for Students

### 1. Break It Into Parts

Choose one hard thing today:
- a math problem
- cleaning your room
- writing a paragraph
- learning a memory verse

Write three smaller steps.

> My problem: ___  
> Step 1: ___  
> Step 2: ___  
> Step 3: ___

---

### 2. Clear or Confusing?

Make two columns:

| Clear | Confusing |
|------|-----------|
| Things I know | Things I need to ask about |

Fill in the chart for one school lesson.

---

### 3. Copywork

Copy this sentence neatly:

> It is not enough to have a good mind; the main thing is to use it well.

Then write one sentence:

> I can use my mind well today by ___.

---

### 4. Method Drawing

Draw Descartes climbing stairs labeled:

1. Clear question
2. Small parts
3. Right order
4. Careful check
5. Better answer

---

## Discussion Questions

1. Why did Descartes like clear thinking?
2. How does breaking a problem into parts help?
3. What does “I think, therefore I am” mean?
4. When is doubt helpful?
5. When can doubt become a problem?
