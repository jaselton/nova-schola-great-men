# George Washington: Student Lesson (Ages 8-10)
## The Leader Who Gave Power Back

---

## Meet George Washington

George Washington was the first president of the United States.

Before that, he was the leader of the American army during the Revolutionary War. His job was very hard. His soldiers were often cold, hungry, tired, and poorly supplied. Sometimes they lost battles. Sometimes it looked as if the whole cause might fail.

Washington did not give up.

He kept working. He learned from mistakes. He encouraged his men. He waited when he needed to wait, and acted boldly when the time was right.

One of his most famous victories came after he crossed the Delaware River in the winter and surprised enemy soldiers at Trenton.

But Washington's greatest lesson came after the war.

He could have tried to keep power. Instead, he gave his command back to Congress. Later, after serving as president, he stepped down again.

That is why we remember him not only as a winner, but as a man who knew that power is for service.

---

## The Hard Part

Washington was not perfect.

He enslaved people at Mount Vernon. That was wrong. When we study Washington, we should tell the truth: he did brave and important things for his country, and he also participated in a serious injustice.

Learning history means learning to see the whole truth.

---

## What to Imitate

**Self-command**
Washington tried to control his temper, fear, pride, and ambition.

**Perseverance**
He kept going when the war was difficult.

**Responsibility**
He took his duties seriously.

**Restraint**
He gave power back instead of keeping it for himself.

---

## What to Guard Against

**Wanting praise too much**
It is good to do honorable things. It is dangerous to care only about being admired.

**Forgetting justice**
A person can do great things and still be wrong about something very important.

**Giving up too soon**
Washington's army survived because he did not quit when things were hard.

---

## Memory Sentence

> A great leader must first learn to lead himself.

Copy this sentence three times in your best handwriting.

---

## Activity: The Power-Giving Chart

Draw two columns.

**Column 1: Keeping Power for Myself**
Write or draw examples:
- Bossing others around
- Refusing to share
- Always needing to win

**Column 2: Using Power to Serve**
Write or draw examples:
- Helping someone smaller
- Letting someone else have a turn
- Doing your duty without bragging

Now answer: Which column is more like Washington at his best?

---

## Discussion Questions

1. Why was it hard for Washington to lead the army?
2. What does it mean to have self-command?
3. Why is giving power back a strong thing to do?
4. How can someone be great and still need correction?
5. What is one duty you can do today without complaining?

---

## Closing Lesson

George Washington teaches us that leaders need courage, patience, and self-control.

He also teaches us that power should not be used for showing off. It should be used to serve.

When you are trusted with responsibility, do your duty. When it is time to give the responsibility back, give it back honorably.
