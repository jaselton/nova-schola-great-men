# Thomas Aquinas for Ages 11-14
## Strong Questions, Fair Answers

---

## The Core Idea

Thomas Aquinas believed that truth deserves careful thinking.

He did not panic when someone raised a hard objection. He did not mock opponents. He tried to state the other side clearly, then answer it with patience and order.

That is one reason he is such an important teacher: he shows students how to disagree without becoming foolish or cruel.

---

## His Formation

Thomas was formed by:

- Early religious education at Monte Cassino
- University study at Naples
- The Dominican life of prayer, poverty, preaching, and study
- The mentorship of Albert the Great
- The scholastic method of questions, objections, answers, and replies

He did not become wise by talent alone. His gifts were trained by habits and teachers.

---

## His Great Method

A typical article in the *Summa Theologiae* often moves like this:

1. Ask a clear question.
2. List serious objections.
3. Give an authority or contrary point.
4. Say, "I answer that..."
5. Reply to each objection.

This method matters because it trains fairness. Thomas wants students to understand before they answer.

---

## His Test of Vocation

Thomas's family wanted a different path for him. They resisted his decision to join the Dominicans.

Thomas stood firm. He did not choose the path that looked most impressive. He chose the path he believed was true.

That teaches an important lesson: sometimes courage looks quiet.

---

## Virtues to Practice

1. **Listen first** — Do not answer a question you have not understood.
2. **Be fair** — State the other side honestly.
3. **Make distinctions** — Many arguments become clearer when you separate ideas carefully.
4. **Stay humble** — Intelligence is a gift to steward, not a weapon to swing.

---

## Debate

**Question:** Should you always answer an objection immediately?

Consider:
- Have you understood it?
- Can you repeat it fairly?
- Do you need more information?
- Are you trying to win, or trying to find truth?

---

## Writing Prompt

Write one page on this question:

**Why is it important to state an opponent's argument fairly before answering it?**

Use Thomas Aquinas as your example.

---

## Discussion Questions

- Why did Thomas's teacher see what other students missed?
- What is the difference between being quiet and being passive?
- How can a student use questions to seek truth instead of attention?
- Why can strong systems become dangerous if they lose humility?
