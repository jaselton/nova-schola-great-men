# Galileo Galilei: One-Week Lesson Plan

---

## Overview

**Target:** All ages (select activities appropriate to your student's level)  
**Time commitment:** 45-60 minutes/day  
**Structure:** 5 days, Monday-Friday  
**Goal:** Introduce Galileo's life, telescope discoveries, mathematical view of nature, conflict over Copernicanism, prudence, and resilience under house arrest

---

## Before You Begin

### Materials Needed
- Notebook or binder
- Pencil and blank paper
- A ball and a simple ramp, board, or book stack
- Optional: binoculars or a small telescope
- Optional: images from *Sidereus Nuncius*
- Optional: short excerpts from *The Assayer* or *Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina*

### Choose Your Age Band
- **Ages 8-10:** Focus on observation, drawing, tools, and careful speech
- **Ages 11-14:** Add evidence charts, Jupiter's moons, and courage versus prudence
- **Ages 15-18:** Add mathematical physics, epistemic authority, rhetoric, and the trial

---

## Monday: The Story

### Morning: Read Together (20-30 min)

**Ages 8-10:** Read the [student lesson ages 8-10](./student-ages-8-10.md) together  
**Ages 11-14:** Read the [student lesson ages 11-14](./student-ages-11-14.md) independently or aloud  
**Ages 15-18:** Assign the [student lesson ages 15-18](./student-ages-15-18.md) as independent reading

**Pause to discuss:**
- Why did Galileo choose mathematics?
- What did he see through the telescope?
- Why is Galileo both admirable and complicated?

---

### Afternoon: Galileo Timeline (20 min)

Create a timeline with:

- 1564: born in Pisa
- Studies medicine, then turns to mathematics
- 1592: begins teaching at Padua
- 1609: improves telescope
- 1610: publishes *Sidereus Nuncius*
- 1616: warned not to defend Copernicanism
- 1632: publishes *Dialogue*
- 1633: trial and house arrest
- 1638: *Two New Sciences*
- 1642: dies at Arcetri

**Reflection:**  
Which moment most changed Galileo's life?

---

## Tuesday: Observation and Instruments

### Morning: Memory Work (5 min)

Younger students memorize:

> Look carefully before you answer.

Older students memorize:

> Observe, measure, reason, and speak with courage and care.

---

### Afternoon: Moon or Object Observation (30-40 min)

Observe the Moon, a plant, a tool, or another object carefully.

Draw it twice:

1. From memory before close observation
2. After careful observation

Then write:

- What did I miss the first time?
- How did careful looking improve my answer?
- What tool would help me see more?

**Older students:** Add a paragraph on how instruments extend human senses but still require interpretation.

---

## Wednesday: Motion and Mathematics

### Morning: Ramp Experiment (30 min)

Use a ball and a ramp.

Try three runs:

1. Low ramp
2. Medium ramp
3. Higher ramp

Record what changes. Younger students can draw; older students can time the motion or measure distance.

**Discussion questions:**
- What stayed the same?
- What changed?
- Why is measuring better than guessing?

---

### Afternoon: The Book of Nature (20-30 min)

Explain Galileo's idea that nature is written in mathematical language.

**Ages 8-10:** Find three things you can count or measure.  
**Ages 11-14:** Write examples of patterns in motion, music, weather, or sports.  
**Ages 15-18:** Read a short excerpt from *The Assayer* and explain the strengths and limits of mathematical description.

---

## Thursday: Courage and Prudence

### Morning: The Galileo Affair (30 min)

Tell the story at the right level:

**Ages 8-10:** Galileo defended ideas that got him in trouble, and he had to live at home under restriction.  
**Ages 11-14:** Galileo supported Copernicus, was warned in 1616, published the *Dialogue* in 1632, and was tried in 1633.  
**Ages 15-18:** Discuss evidence, biblical interpretation, institutional authority, Reformation-era pressure, rhetoric, and Pope Urban VIII.

**Important correction:** Galileo was not executed. He lived under house arrest and continued writing.

---

### Afternoon: Courage vs. Recklessness Debate (25-30 min)

Topic:

> Galileo was right to push his argument as strongly as he did.

**Arguments for:**
- Evidence mattered.
- Old explanations were failing.
- Fear should not govern truth.

**Arguments against:**
- Tone affects whether truth is heard.
- Institutions react badly to humiliation.
- Prudence can protect the work.

After debate, each student writes:

> Galileo's courage was admirable because ___. His prudence was limited because ___.

---

## Friday: Synthesis and Application

### Morning: Evidence Before Opinion (30 min)

Choose one current claim from school, family life, science, sports, or history.

Make an evidence chart:

1. Claim
2. What I observed
3. What I measured or sourced
4. What conclusion is justified
5. What I still do not know

**Ages 15-18:** Add a section on how you would communicate the conclusion prudently to someone who disagrees.

---

### Afternoon: Final Reflection (20-30 min)

Choose one prompt:

**Ages 8-10:**  
Draw Galileo with a telescope and write: “Look carefully before you answer.”

**Ages 11-14:**  
Write one paragraph: “What Galileo teaches me about evidence and courage.”

**Ages 15-18:**  
Write an essay outline: “Courage needs prudence: Galileo as a case study.”

---

## Optional Extensions

### Astronomy
Observe the Moon over several nights and track its changing shape.

### History
Place Galileo on a timeline with Leonardo, Erasmus, Descartes, and Newton.

### Science
Research how telescopes work and draw a labeled diagram.

### Rhetoric
Rewrite one sharp argument in a more prudent tone without weakening the truth.
