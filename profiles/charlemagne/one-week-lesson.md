# Charlemagne: One-Week Lesson Plan

---

## Overview

**Target:** All ages (select activities appropriate to your student's level)  
**Time commitment:** 45-60 minutes/day  
**Structure:** 5 days, Monday-Friday  
**Goal:** Introduce Charlemagne's life, reforms, and moral lessons through story, source work, writing, mapping, and discussion

---

## Before You Begin

### Materials Needed
- Notebook or binder
- Pen or pencil for copywork
- Blank map of early medieval Europe or access to an atlas
- Optional images of Carolingian manuscripts / Carolingian minuscule
- Optional excerpts from Einhard's *Life of Charlemagne* and the *Admonitio Generalis*

### Choose Your Age Band
- **Ages 8-10:** Focus on story, handwriting, schools, and using strength well
- **Ages 11-14:** Add conquest vs. reform, mapping, and moral judgment
- **Ages 15-18:** Add primary source analysis, debate, and ethical evaluation

---

## Monday: The Story

### Morning: Read Together (20-30 min)

**Ages 8-10:** Read [student lesson ages 8-10](./student-ages-8-10.md) together  
**Ages 11-14:** Read [student lesson ages 11-14](./student-ages-11-14.md) independently or aloud  
**Ages 15-18:** Assign [student lesson ages 15-18](./student-ages-15-18.md) as independent reading

**Pause to discuss:**
- What kind of world did Charlemagne inherit?
- Why was learning weak or fragile?
- Why did Charlemagne need scholars?
- What makes his story morally complicated?

---

### Afternoon: First Reflection (15-20 min)

**Ages 8-10:** Draw Charlemagne's school. Include a teacher, students, books, and rules.

**Ages 11-14:** Write a half-page answer: "Why does a kingdom need schools?"

**Ages 15-18:** Write a thesis sentence answering: "Was Charlemagne's deepest legacy political or educational?"

---

## Tuesday: Books, Writing, and Civilization

### Morning: Copywork (15 min)

Copy slowly and carefully:

**Ages 8-10:**
> A good ruler helps his people become wise and good.

**Ages 11-14:**
> Let schools be established in which boys may learn to read, and let books be corrected carefully.

**Ages 15-18:**
> The correction of books, the training of clergy, and the founding of schools reveal that Charlemagne understood ignorance as a public danger.

After copying, compare first and final lines for clarity.

---

### Afternoon: Manuscript Accuracy Exercise (30-40 min)

1. Parent writes or prints a short paragraph.
2. Student copies it by hand.
3. Student copies the copy.
4. Compare the second copy to the original.

**Discuss:**
- What mistakes appeared?
- Why would errors matter in Scripture, law, or prayer books?
- How did clear handwriting help preserve learning?

**Older students:** Research or inspect an image of Carolingian minuscule. What makes it readable?

---

## Wednesday: Conquest and Reform

### Morning: Map Work (20-30 min)

Mark on a map:
- Aachen
- Rome
- Saxony
- Lombardy / northern Italy
- Pyrenees / Roncesvalles
- Danube / Avar region

**Questions:**
- Which areas were farthest from Charlemagne's center?
- Why would travel and communication be difficult?
- Why did he need inspectors and local officials?

---

### Afternoon: Conquest vs. Reform Chart (20-30 min)

Create two columns:

**Conquest** | **Reform**
---|---
Wins territory | Builds schools
Requires armies | Requires teachers
Can happen quickly | Takes generations
Can create fear | Can build understanding

Add at least five more rows.

**Discussion:** Which column better explains why Charlemagne still matters?

---

## Thursday: Moral Limits of Power

### Morning: The Saxony Question (20-30 min)

Explain age-appropriately:

Charlemagne fought the Saxons for decades. They resisted Frankish rule and Christianity. Charlemagne responded with harsh punishments and sometimes tried to force Christian practice. This was wrong because faith cannot be forced into the heart.

**Ages 8-10:** Discuss: "Can you make someone good by scaring them?"

**Ages 11-14:** Write: "What is the difference between making someone obey and helping someone become good?"

**Ages 15-18:** Prepare arguments for and against this claim: "Charlemagne's greatness is permanently compromised by his actions in Saxony."

---

### Afternoon: Counsel Charlemagne (30 min)

Students write a letter to Charlemagne as if they were Alcuin.

The letter should include:
- Praise for his desire to teach and reform
- Warning against forced conversion
- A better plan for teaching the Saxons
- One Scripture or moral principle if appropriate

**Younger students:** Dictate the letter orally or write 5-6 sentences.

---

## Friday: Synthesis and Presentation

### Morning: Review Discussion (20-30 min)

Ask:
1. What did Charlemagne build besides an empire?
2. Why did he care about books and schools?
3. Who was Alcuin, and why did he matter?
4. What should we imitate in Charlemagne?
5. What should we reject?
6. What small disciplines preserve civilization today?

---

### Afternoon: Final Project (45-60 min)

Choose one:

**Ages 8-10: Poster**  
Make a poster titled "Charlemagne: The King Who Wanted Schools." Include three good things he did and one warning from his life.

**Ages 11-14: Two-Paragraph Judgment**  
Paragraph 1: Charlemagne's virtues and achievements.  
Paragraph 2: Charlemagne's failures and warnings.

**Ages 15-18: Essay or Seminar**  
Prompt:
> Can political power form virtue? Use Charlemagne as your central example.

Requirements:
- Mention the Carolingian Renaissance
- Mention Alcuin
- Mention Saxony
- Distinguish outward order from inward formation

---

## Memory Work

Choose one:

> "To have another language is to possess a second soul."  
> — Attributed to Charlemagne

or

> "He cultivated the liberal arts most studiously."  
> — Einhard on Charlemagne

---

## Assessment Checklist

By the end of the week, the student should be able to explain:

- Who Charlemagne was
- Why the year 800 mattered
- What the Carolingian Renaissance was
- Why schools and corrected books mattered
- Who Alcuin was
- Why forced conversion is morally wrong
- What virtues to imitate and what dangers to avoid
