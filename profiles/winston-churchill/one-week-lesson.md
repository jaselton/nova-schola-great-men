# Winston Churchill: One-Week Lesson Plan
## The Voice That Would Not Surrender

---

## Overview

This one-week study teaches Churchill through the theme of truthful courage: words, preparation, failure, prudence, democratic resolve, and moral judgment.

**Formation Aim:** Students should understand that courage tells the truth, refuses wrongful surrender, and remains accountable to moral limits.

---

## Before You Begin

### Materials Needed

- Student lesson for the appropriate age band
- Notebook or binder page for Churchill
- Map of Europe and Britain in 1940
- Short excerpts from Churchill's May 13, June 4, or June 18, 1940 speeches
- Optional: audio recording of a Churchill speech

### Choose Your Age Band

- **Ages 8-10:** Focus on bravery, truthful words, and not giving up.
- **Ages 11-14:** Add Gallipoli, prudence, warning, and failure.
- **Ages 15-18:** Add oratory, empire, democratic limits, and moral judgment.

---

## Monday: The Story

### Morning: Read Together (20-30 min)

Read the profile overview and the appropriate student lesson.

Ask:
- What danger did Britain face in 1940?
- Why did Churchill's words matter?
- Was Churchill perfect? Why not?

### Afternoon: Start a Churchill Notebook (15-20 min)

Create a page with four boxes:

1. **Young Churchill** — school, Sandhurst, soldier, writer
2. **Failure** — Gallipoli and recovery
3. **Warning** — Hitler and the 1930s
4. **1940** — Prime Minister, speeches, resistance

Younger students may draw pictures. Older students should write three bullet points per box.

---

## Tuesday: Truthful Courage

### Morning: Memory Work (5 min)

Younger students:

> Tell the truth. Do not give up.

Older students:

> I have nothing to offer but blood, toil, tears and sweat.

Discuss: Why would a leader begin by saying something so hard?

### Afternoon: Encouragement Without Lying (25-35 min)

Give the student a hard situation:
- A difficult exam
- A team losing badly
- A family facing a move
- A friend who failed at something

Write two responses:

1. **False comfort:** Pretend everything is easy.
2. **Truthful courage:** Name the difficulty and give the next right action.

Discuss which one is more like Churchill and why.

---

## Wednesday: Failure and Prudence

### Morning: Gallipoli (20-30 min)

Teach simply:

In the First World War, Churchill supported a bold plan called the Dardanelles/Gallipoli campaign. It was meant to help the Allies, but it failed terribly and cost many lives. Churchill lost office.

Discuss:
- Why are bold plans attractive?
- What must leaders check before taking risks?
- How should a person recover after a serious failure?

### Afternoon: Bold or Reckless Chart (25-35 min)

Make two columns:

**Bold** | **Reckless**

Add examples from daily life:
- Speaking truth respectfully
- Trying a hard new skill
- Ignoring safety rules
- Taking charge without listening
- Making a plan and accepting correction

Older students should add Gallipoli and 1940 to the chart and explain why one was failure and the other necessary courage.

---

## Thursday: The Speeches

### Morning: Speech Study (30-40 min)

Read a short excerpt from one 1940 speech.

Mark:
- Where Churchill names danger
- Where he calls for duty
- Where repetition strengthens resolve
- Where he points to history or future generations

Younger students can underline brave words. Older students should identify rhetorical devices.

### Afternoon: Write a Five-Sentence Speech (25-35 min)

Prompt:

“Write a short speech to encourage people facing something hard.”

Requirements:
1. Tell the truth about the difficulty.
2. Explain why the effort matters.
3. Remind the listeners of one strength.
4. Give a next action.
5. End with courage.

Have the student read it aloud. Discuss tone: Was it honest? Was it strengthening?

---

## Friday: Judgment and Synthesis

### Morning: The Whole Man (25-35 min)

Teach plainly:

Churchill helped defend liberty against Hitler. But he also believed strongly in the British Empire and did not always see the freedom claims of colonized peoples clearly.

Ask:
- Can a person do something very noble and still be wrong in other ways?
- Why is hero worship dangerous?
- Why is cynical dismissal also dangerous?

### Afternoon: Final Project (45-60 min)

Choose one:

**Ages 8-10:** Draw Churchill speaking to Britain in 1940. Add the sentence: “Tell the truth. Do not give up.”

**Ages 11-14:** Write one page: “How did Churchill show courage, and what warnings does his life give us?”

**Ages 15-18:** Write a short essay: “Churchill should be admired and judged.” Include 1940, Gallipoli, and empire.

---

## Optional Weekend Extensions

- Listen to an audio excerpt of a Churchill speech and discuss delivery.
- Compare Churchill with George Washington on power and democratic limits.
- Compare Churchill with Cicero on the public use of speech.
- Map Europe in June 1940 and explain why Britain's choice mattered.
- Research the 1945 British election and write a paragraph on democratic accountability.
