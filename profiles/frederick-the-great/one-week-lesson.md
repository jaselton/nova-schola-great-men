# Frederick the Great: One-Week Lesson Plan

---

## Overview

**Target:** All ages (select activities appropriate to your student's level)  
**Time commitment:** 45-60 minutes/day  
**Structure:** 5 days, Monday-Friday  
**Goal:** Introduce Frederick's life, discipline, reforms, wars, and moral lessons about power and service

---

## Before You Begin

### Materials Needed
- Notebook or history journal
- Pencil
- Map of eighteenth-century Europe, or blank map of central Europe
- Colored pencils
- Optional: image of Frederick, Sanssouci, Prussian soldiers, and a map of Silesia
- Optional: short flute recording connected to Frederick's musical world

### Choose Your Age Band
- **Ages 8-10:** Focus on leadership as service, work before privilege, and strength governed by right
- **Ages 11-14:** Add Prussia, Austria, Silesia, discipline, and the difference between success and justice
- **Ages 15-18:** Add enlightened absolutism, primary source analysis, and comparison with Washington or Napoleon

---

## Monday: The Story

### Morning: Read Together (20-30 min)

**Ages 8-10:** Read the [student lesson ages 8-10](./student-ages-8-10.md) together  
**Ages 11-14:** Read the [student lesson ages 11-14](./student-ages-11-14.md) — or have them read independently  
**Ages 15-18:** Assign the [student lesson ages 15-18](./student-ages-15-18.md) as independent reading

**As you read, pause to discuss:**
- “What did Frederick love besides ruling?”
- “What does it mean for a leader to be a servant?”
- “Why is Frederick morally complicated?”

---

### Afternoon: Start a Frederick Notebook (15-20 min)

**Activity:** Set up a notebook on leadership and power

Write on the first page:

> Frederick Notebook — Power, Service, and Justice

Copy this sentence:

> A leader serves before he is served.

Then write or draw two pictures:

1. Frederick as a working king
2. Frederick as a warning about power

---

## Tuesday: Formation and Discipline

### Morning: Memory Work (5 min)

**All ages:** Memorize:

> A leader serves before he is served.

**Older students may also memorize:**

> “The sovereign is the first servant of the State.”  
> — Frederick II

---

### Afternoon: Formation Chart (30-40 min)

Make a chart with three columns:

1. **Event**
2. **How it formed Frederick**
3. **Virtue or danger produced**

Include:
- strict father
- love of music and books
- failed escape
- Katte's execution
- Rheinsberg reading and writing
- inheriting Prussia's army and bureaucracy

**Ages 8-10:** Use drawings and simple phrases.  
**Ages 11-14:** Write one sentence per row.  
**Ages 15-18:** Add a paragraph: “Formation explains but does not excuse.”

---

## Wednesday: Map and Moral Judgment

### Morning: Map Prussia, Austria, and Silesia (30 min)

Find or draw central Europe. Mark:

- Prussia
- Austria / Habsburg lands
- Silesia
- Saxony
- France
- Russia
- Britain / Hanover if using a wider map

Discuss:
- Why would Silesia matter strategically and economically?
- Why might Frederick see opportunity in 1740?
- Why is opportunity not the same as right?

---

### Afternoon: Two Questions Exercise (20-30 min)

Write these two questions:

1. Did it work?
2. Was it right?

Apply them to:
- Frederick taking Silesia
- a student using strength to get his way
- a team captain choosing what helps the team but hurts one teammate

**Ages 15-18 extension:**  
Write 300 words on why history must judge both outcomes and justice.

---

## Thursday: Culture, Reform, and the First Servant

### Morning: Culture and Command (20-30 min)

Discuss Frederick as:
- soldier
- king
- administrator
- flute player
- reader and writer
- builder of Sanssouci

Ask:
- Why do we separate strength and culture?
- Did culture make Frederick morally better automatically?

Optional: listen to a short eighteenth-century flute piece and look at an image of Sanssouci.

---

### Afternoon: Primary Source Copywork (15-25 min)

**Ages 8-10:**
> A leader serves before he is served.

Copy 3 times. Then do one useful job.

**Ages 11-14:**
> “The sovereign is the first servant of the State.”

Copy once. Then write: What should a servant-king do each day?

**Ages 15-18:**
> “The sovereign is the first servant of the State.”

Write a one-page analysis:
- What is noble about this idea?
- What is dangerous about serving the state above all else?
- How does Silesia test the idea?

---

## Friday: Synthesis

### Morning: Character Discussion (20-30 min)

Discuss Frederick's virtues and flaws.

**Virtues:** industry, discipline, courage, strategic clarity, cultivation  
**Flaws:** aggression, coldness, cynicism, ambition, pride of success

Ask:
- Which virtue should you imitate?
- Which flaw should you guard against?
- What should rule power?

---

### Afternoon: Final Project (45-60 min)

Choose one:

**Ages 8-10: Servant Leader Poster**
- Draw a leader doing work for others
- Add: “A leader serves by ___.”

**Ages 11-14: Frederick Judgment Chart**
- Make two columns: “Great” and “Dangerous”
- Put at least five items in each
- Write one paragraph explaining your judgment

**Ages 15-18: Analytical Essay**
- Write 1,000-1,400 words:

> Frederick the Great shows that disciplined leadership is not enough; power must be governed by justice.

Include Silesia, the Seven Years' War, “first servant of the State,” and one comparison.

---

## Optional Weekend Extensions

- Compare Frederick with George Washington on power and restraint
- Compare Frederick with Napoleon on military genius and ambition
- Research Sanssouci and discuss what it says about Frederick's self-image
- Read an excerpt from *Anti-Machiavel*
- Make a timeline from 1712 to 1786

---

## Parent Closing Script

“Frederick teaches us that leadership is not laziness or decoration. A leader works. He prepares. He serves. But Frederick also warns us that strength and success are not enough. Power must answer to justice, or it becomes dangerous.”
