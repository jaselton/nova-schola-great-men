# Frederick the Great: Student Lesson (Ages 8-10)
## The King Who Worked

---

## Meet Frederick

Frederick was a prince in Prussia. He liked music, books, and beautiful ideas. He played the flute and loved to read.

But his father was very strict. His father wanted Frederick to be tough, obedient, and ready to command soldiers. Frederick's childhood was not easy. He learned discipline, but he also learned fear.

When Frederick became king, he did not spend all day relaxing in a palace. He worked very hard. He read reports. He inspected soldiers. He wrote letters. He tried to make the courts and government work better. He said a ruler should be the “first servant of the State.”

That means: the leader should serve first.

---

## The Hard Part

Frederick was brave and hardworking, but he was not always right.

Soon after he became king, he took a rich land called Silesia from Austria. This helped make Prussia stronger, but it also helped start years of war.

So we should not say, “Frederick won, so Frederick was right.” Winning and being right are not the same thing.

---

## What to Imitate

Frederick can teach you:

- Do your work before taking your reward
- Prepare before trouble comes
- Be brave when things are difficult
- Learn more than one skill: Frederick was a soldier and a musician
- Remember that leaders should help others

---

## What to Guard Against

Frederick also warns you:

- Do not use strength to take what is not yours
- Do not think winning proves you were good
- Do not become cold toward other people
- Do not say noble words and then do selfish things

---

## Memory Sentence

> A leader serves before he is served.

Copy it neatly three times.

---

## Activity: The Leader's Work List

Imagine you are responsible for a small kingdom, classroom, family, or team.

Make a list of five jobs a good leader should do before enjoying privileges.

Example:

1. Listen to problems.
2. Make sure everyone has what they need.
3. Do the hardest job first.
4. Keep promises.
5. Protect the weak.

Now choose one real job you can do today.

---

## Discussion Questions

1. What did Frederick like besides war and ruling?
2. What does it mean to be the first servant?
3. Why is winning not the same as being right?
4. What is one way you can serve before being served?

---

## Closing Lesson

Frederick teaches us that leadership is work. A king, parent, teacher, or captain should not just enjoy being important. A leader should serve.

But Frederick also teaches us to be careful. Strong people must ask, “Is this right?” before they ask, “Can I win?”
