# Alcuin of York: One-Week Lesson Plan

---

## Overview

**Target:** All ages (select activities appropriate to your student's level)  
**Time commitment:** 45-60 minutes/day  
**Structure:** 5 days, Monday-Friday  
**Goal:** Introduce Alcuin's life, educational vision, and moral lessons through story, copywork, dialogue, curriculum design, and discussion

---

## Before You Begin

### Materials Needed
- Notebook or binder
- Pen or pencil for copywork
- A short paragraph for copying practice
- Optional images of medieval manuscripts / Carolingian minuscule
- Optional excerpts from Alcuin's letters or educational dialogues
- Five favorite books from your home library

### Choose Your Age Band
- **Ages 8-10:** Focus on teacher, books, careful copying, and helping others learn
- **Ages 11-14:** Add York, palace school, command vs. counsel, and grammar as truth-care
- **Ages 15-18:** Add liberal arts, Saxony, scholar-at-court debate, and source analysis

---

## Monday: The Story

### Morning: Read Together (20-30 min)

**Ages 8-10:** Read [student lesson ages 8-10](./student-ages-8-10.md) together  
**Ages 11-14:** Read [student lesson ages 11-14](./student-ages-11-14.md) independently or aloud  
**Ages 15-18:** Assign [student lesson ages 15-18](./student-ages-15-18.md) as independent reading

**Pause to discuss:**
- Why was York important?
- Why did Charlemagne need Alcuin?
- What can teachers do that kings cannot?
- Why do words and books matter?

---

### Afternoon: First Reflection (15-20 min)

**Ages 8-10:** Draw Alcuin teaching Charlemagne. Include books, students, and a teacher's chair.

**Ages 11-14:** Write a half-page answer: "How can a teacher change history?"

**Ages 15-18:** Write a thesis sentence answering: "Was Alcuin's influence primarily intellectual, moral, political, or religious?"

---

## Tuesday: Books and Careful Words

### Morning: Copywork (15 min)

Copy slowly and carefully:

**Ages 8-10:**
> A teacher helps others love what is true.

**Ages 11-14:**
> Clear words protect truth, and careless words confuse the mind.

**Ages 15-18:**
> Alcuin's grammar was not pedantry; it was a discipline of attention ordered toward truth, worship, and judgment.

Compare the first and final copies for clarity.

---

### Afternoon: Manuscript Accuracy Exercise (30-40 min)

1. Choose a short paragraph from a good book.
2. Student copies it by hand.
3. Student copies the copy.
4. Compare the second copy to the original.

**Discuss:**
- What mistakes appeared?
- What kinds of mistakes would matter most in Scripture, law, or poetry?
- Why did medieval schools care about copying and grammar?

**Older students:** Inspect an image of Carolingian minuscule. What design choices make it readable?

---

## Wednesday: The School

### Morning: Build the Palace School (30 min)

Students design Charlemagne's palace school.

Include:
- Subjects taught
- Daily schedule
- Rules for teachers
- Rules for students
- One habit of prayer, memory, or copywork
- One way to train future teachers

**Ages 8-10:** Draw the school and label parts.  
**Ages 11-14:** Make a one-page plan.  
**Ages 15-18:** Build a curriculum organized by trivium, quadrivium, and moral formation.

---

### Afternoon: Teach by Dialogue (20-30 min)

The student teaches someone else using questions.

Steps:
1. Pick a simple topic.
2. Ask questions instead of lecturing.
3. Let the learner discover the answer.
4. Correct gently.
5. Reflect afterward.

**Reflection:** What did questions do that direct telling did not do?

---

## Thursday: Counsel and Power

### Morning: Command vs. Counsel (20-30 min)

Create a chart:

**Command** | **Counsel**
---|---
Orders action | Shapes judgment
Works quickly | Works slowly
Uses authority | Uses persuasion
Can force obedience | Cannot force love of truth

Add at least five rows.

**Discuss:** Which belonged more to Charlemagne? Which belonged more to Alcuin? Which is more important for education?

---

### Afternoon: The Saxony Question (30 min)

Explain age-appropriately:

Charlemagne sometimes confused conquest with Christian formation. Alcuin warned that people must be taught before being expected to live the faith, and that force cannot create true belief.

**Ages 8-10:** Discuss: "Can fear make someone love what is good?"

**Ages 11-14:** Write: "What should a teacher say when a powerful person is wrong?"

**Ages 15-18:** Prepare arguments for a seminar: "Scholars should serve rulers only if they remain free to correct them."

---

## Friday: Synthesis and Presentation

### Morning: Review Discussion (20-30 min)

Ask:
1. Who was Alcuin?
2. Why did York matter?
3. Why did Charlemagne need him?
4. What are the liberal arts for?
5. Why do grammar and copying matter?
6. What should we imitate in Alcuin?
7. What risks should scholars near power avoid?

---

### Afternoon: Final Project (45-60 min)

Choose one:

**Ages 8-10: Poster**  
Make a poster titled "Alcuin: The Teacher Who Helped a King." Include three things teachers do and one reason books matter.

**Ages 11-14: Two-Paragraph Judgment**  
Paragraph 1: What Alcuin gave Charlemagne's empire.  
Paragraph 2: Why Alcuin had to warn Charlemagne.

**Ages 15-18: Essay or Seminar**  
Prompt:
> What can education do for civilization that political power cannot do?

Requirements:
- Mention York
- Mention Charlemagne's palace school
- Mention grammar or liberal arts
- Mention Alcuin's counsel against coercion
- Distinguish command from formation

---

## Memory Work

Choose one:

> A teacher helps others love what is true.

or

> Clear words protect truth.

or for older students:

> Power can command a school into existence; only teachers can make it a place of formation.

---

## Assessment Checklist

By the end of the week, the student should be able to explain:

- Who Alcuin was
- Why York's school and library mattered
- Why Charlemagne invited him
- What the palace school was for
- Why grammar and careful copying mattered
- Why faith cannot be forced
- How teachers shape civilization quietly
