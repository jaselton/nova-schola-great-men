# Thomas Jefferson: Student Lesson (Ages 8-10)
## The Man Who Wrote Big Words

---

## Meet Thomas Jefferson

Thomas Jefferson was one of America's Founders.

He wrote many of the words in the Declaration of Independence. The Declaration told the world that the American colonies were becoming a new country. It also said something very important:

> “All men are created equal.”

Those words helped people understand that liberty is not just a wish. It is a truth about human beings.

Jefferson loved books, languages, buildings, gardens, music, and learning. He believed a free country needed people who could read, think, and make wise choices.

Later, he became the third president of the United States. As president, he helped buy a huge territory called Louisiana, which made the country much larger. Near the end of his life, he helped build the University of Virginia because he wanted students to keep learning.

---

## The Hard Part

Jefferson was not perfect. He did something very wrong.

He enslaved people at Monticello, his home in Virginia. That means he wrote about liberty while denying liberty to other people.

We should tell the truth about both things: Jefferson wrote words that helped the cause of freedom, and he also failed to live those words fully.

History teaches us to love the truth more than pretending.

---

## What to Imitate

**Love of learning**
Jefferson kept reading, studying, and asking questions.

**Careful words**
He used words to explain important truths.

**Respect for conscience**
He believed people should not be forced to believe by the government.

**Building schools**
He wanted young people to learn so they could be free and responsible.

---

## What to Guard Against

**Saying without doing**
Good words should become good actions.

**Ignoring wrongs nearby**
It is easy to talk about justice far away while missing injustice close to home.

**Pride in being smart**
Learning should make us more truthful, not more proud.

---

## Memory Sentence

> True words should become true actions.

Copy this sentence three times in your best handwriting.

---

## Activity: Words and Actions

Fold a paper in half.

On one side write: **True Words**  
On the other side write: **True Actions**

Under **True Words**, copy:
- All men are created equal.
- People should be free to think and worship.
- A free country needs learning.

Under **True Actions**, write or draw:
- Treating others fairly
- Telling the truth
- Letting others learn
- Standing up when someone is treated wrongly

Now ask: Which is harder—saying true words or living them?

---

## Discussion Questions

1. What important words did Jefferson write?
2. Why are words powerful?
3. Why did Jefferson care about schools?
4. What was wrong about Jefferson enslaving people?
5. What is one true thing you can practice today?

---

## Closing Lesson

Thomas Jefferson teaches us that words can be powerful. A true sentence can help a whole country understand justice.

But he also teaches us that saying true words is not enough. We must try to live by the truth we speak.
