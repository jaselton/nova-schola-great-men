# Napoleon Bonaparte
## The Commander Who Turned Revolution into Empire

**Born:** August 15, 1769, Ajaccio, Corsica  
**Died:** May 5, 1821, St. Helena  
**Era:** Modern / Revolutionary and Napoleonic Europe  
**Domain:** Military Command, Law, Statecraft, Empire

---

## The One-Minute Story

Napoleon Bonaparte was a young Corsican artillery officer who rose through the chaos of the French Revolution by ability, discipline, and daring.

He won fame at Toulon, dazzled Europe in the Italian campaign, seized power in the coup of 18 Brumaire, became First Consul, and then crowned himself Emperor of the French. He reorganized France, supported the Civil Code, strengthened administration, founded lycées, and opened careers to talent.

But Napoleon could not stop expanding power. The man who promised order after revolution became a ruler of censorship, dynasty, conquest, and war. His victories made him believe he could bend Europe to his will. Russia proved otherwise. Waterloo ended the imperial dream.

He teaches students that disciplined talent can change history—but ability without moral limits can destroy what it builds.

---

## Why He Matters for Your Child

**Napoleon teaches:**
- Discipline, study, and concentration of effort
- Strategic thinking: finding the decisive point
- Merit and administrative order
- That laws and institutions can outlast victories

**But also warns against:**
- Ambition without limits
- Confusing order with liberty
- Glory becoming an addiction
- Letting success become proof that no one may correct you

---

## Formation Signature

### Virtues (What to Imitate)
- **Discipline** — technical mastery, reading, preparation, and work
- **Strategic imagination** — seeing the decisive point before others
- **Energy** — extraordinary capacity for action and organization
- **Merit** — honoring ability over inherited privilege
- **Institution-building** — law, schools, administration, and finance

### Risks (What to Guard Against)
- **Ambition** — desire for greatness becoming desire for domination
- **Pride** — success closing the ear to counsel
- **Control** — order purchased at the expense of liberty
- **Glory addiction** — needing victory to justify rule
- **Overreach** — ignoring limits of geography, logistics, and human endurance

### How He Was Formed
- **Corsican outsider status** → hunger, seriousness, and insecurity
- **Military education** → mathematics, artillery, calculation, and discipline
- **French Revolution** → opened promotion by ability
- **Italian campaign** → confirmed his genius and fed his ambition
- **Consulate and Empire** → gave him power to organize and power to abuse

## Quick Practice Ideas

**Ages 8-10:**
- Practice one skill carefully for 15 minutes and record improvement
- Draw a map showing Corsica, France, Russia, and St. Helena
- Memorize: “Talent must obey truth.”

**Ages 11-14:**
- Make a chart: order / liberty / ambition
- Study a simple battle map and identify the decisive point
- Discuss: When does confidence become pride?

**Ages 15-18:**
- Debate: Was Napoleon heir to the Revolution or betrayer of it?
- Analyze the Napoleonic Code as order without full liberty
- Essay: “Why did Napoleon's strengths become dangerous?”
