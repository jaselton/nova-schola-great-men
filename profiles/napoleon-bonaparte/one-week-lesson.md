# Napoleon Bonaparte: One-Week Lesson Plan
## The Commander Who Turned Revolution into Empire

---

## Overview

This one-week study teaches Napoleon through the theme of disciplined ability and dangerous ambition: practice, strategy, order, law, empire, and limits.

**Formation Aim:** Students should understand that talent must be disciplined—and disciplined talent must still obey truth.

---

## Before You Begin

### Materials Needed

- Student lesson for the appropriate age band
- Notebook or binder page for Napoleon
- Map of Europe, Corsica, Russia, and St. Helena
- Short summary or excerpt on the Civil Code
- Optional: images of Napoleon, the coronation, Austerlitz map, Russia retreat map

### Choose Your Age Band

- **Ages 8-10:** Focus on practice, pride, and limits.
- **Ages 11-14:** Add strategy, order, liberty, and overreach.
- **Ages 15-18:** Add Revolution, Civil Code, empire, censorship, and Washington comparison.

---

## Monday: The Story

### Morning: Read Together (20-30 min)

Read the profile overview and the appropriate student lesson.

Ask:
- Where did Napoleon come from?
- How did discipline help him rise?
- What made his ambition dangerous?

### Afternoon: Start a Napoleon Notebook (15-20 min)

Create a page with four boxes:

1. **Student and Officer** — Corsica, military school, artillery
2. **General** — Toulon, Italy, speed, strategy
3. **Ruler** — First Consul, Civil Code, schools, administration
4. **Warning** — emperor, war, Russia, Waterloo

Younger students may draw pictures. Older students should write three bullet points per box.

---

## Tuesday: Discipline and Strategy

### Morning: Memory Work (5 min)

Copy or recite:

> Talent must obey truth.

Older students may add:

> Tactical skill cannot redeem strategic folly.

### Afternoon: Decisive Point Exercise (25-40 min)

Explain that Napoleon often looked for the decisive point: the place where action mattered most.

Give the student a simple problem:
- A messy room
- A hard math topic
- A sports skill
- A family schedule problem

Ask:
1. What is the decisive point?
2. What action would help most?
3. What would be wasted effort?

Older students may compare this with Toulon or the Italian campaign.

---

## Wednesday: Order and Law

### Morning: The Civil Code (20-30 min)

Teach simply:

Napoleon supported a unified Civil Code for France. It helped replace confusing local laws with clearer national law. It protected property and equality before law for male citizens, but it did not create full liberty and treated women unequally within the family.

Discuss:
- Why are clear laws important?
- Why is equality before law important?
- Why is law not enough if liberty is missing?

### Afternoon: Build a Just Rulebook (30-45 min)

Ask the student to design five rules for a small school, club, or household.

Each rule must be:
- Clear
- Fair
- Possible to obey
- Protective of people, not just efficient

Older students should write a paragraph: “Can order become unjust?”

---

## Thursday: Empire and Overreach

### Morning: Map Napoleon's World (25-35 min)

Use a map to find:
- Corsica
- Paris
- Egypt
- Russia
- Waterloo
- St. Helena

Discuss:
- What does distance teach about power?
- Why would supply and weather matter in war?
- Why could Russia defeat Napoleon without needing to win one perfect battle?

### Afternoon: Strengths Become Dangers Chart (25-35 min)

Make two columns:

**Strength** | **When It Goes Wrong**

Add examples:
- Confidence | Pride
- Speed | Recklessness
- Order | Control
- Ambition | Domination
- Victory | Addiction to glory

Older students should write a paragraph: “Which strength most contributed to Napoleon's fall?”

---

## Friday: Moral Judgment and Synthesis

### Morning: Washington Comparison (20-30 min)

Teach plainly:

George Washington won military glory and gave power back. Napoleon won military glory and crowned himself emperor.

Ask:
- Why is surrendering power difficult?
- Which is greater: winning command or limiting oneself?
- What does this comparison teach about character?

### Afternoon: Final Project (45-60 min)

Choose one:

**Ages 8-10:** Draw two Napoleons: the disciplined student and the proud emperor. Label each with a lesson.

**Ages 11-14:** Write one page: “How did Napoleon's strengths become dangerous?”

**Ages 15-18:** Write a short essay: “Napoleon preserved revolutionary order while betraying revolutionary liberty.” Include the Civil Code, empire, and Russia or Waterloo.

---

## Optional Weekend Extensions

- Compare Napoleon with Alexander the Great.
- Research one article of the Civil Code and explain its purpose.
- Map the retreat from Russia and write a logistics explanation.
- Debate: Was Napoleon's greatest legacy military, legal, or cautionary?

---

## Parent Closing Script

“Napoleon shows us how powerful disciplined talent can be. He studied, practiced, organized, commanded, and built institutions that lasted. But he also shows us that talent is not enough. Ambition must be governed by truth, and power must accept limits. From Napoleon we learn to work hard, think strategically, build order—and fear pride.”
