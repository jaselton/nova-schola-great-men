# Lorenzo de' Medici: One-Week Lesson Plan

---

## Overview

**Target:** All ages (select activities appropriate to your student's level)  
**Time commitment:** 45-60 minutes/day  
**Structure:** 5 days, Monday-Friday  
**Goal:** Introduce Lorenzo's life, patronage, political courage, and moral ambiguity through story, discussion, and practical stewardship

---

## Before You Begin

### Materials Needed
- Notebook or binder
- Blank paper for mapping Lorenzo's circle
- Colored pencils or markers
- Access to images of Florence, Botticelli, Michelangelo's early sculpture, or Medici architecture
- One shelf, table, or wall space for a small beauty-and-learning display
- Optional: a short excerpt from Lorenzo's "Triumph of Bacchus and Ariadne"

### Choose Your Age Band
- **Ages 8-10:** Focus on patronage, generosity, and helping good things grow
- **Ages 11-14:** Add Florence, informal power, and the Pazzi crisis
- **Ages 15-18:** Add republican form vs. substance, institutional durability, and cultural ambiguity

---

## Monday: The Story

### Morning: Read Together (20-30 min)

**Ages 8-10:** Read the [student lesson ages 8-10](./student-ages-8-10.md) together  
**Ages 11-14:** Read the [student lesson ages 11-14](./student-ages-11-14.md) independently or aloud  
**Ages 15-18:** Assign the [student lesson ages 15-18](./student-ages-15-18.md) as independent reading

**Pause to discuss:**
- What is a patron?
- How did Lorenzo help Florence become a place of learning and beauty?
- What dangers came with Medici power?

---

### Afternoon: Draw Lorenzo's Circle (20 min)

Place "Lorenzo" in the center of a page. Around him, add:

- Artist
- Poet
- Philosopher
- Sculptor
- Architect
- Banker
- Citizen
- Diplomat

Draw lines showing how each person might help the others.

**Reflection:**  
Greatness often grows in circles. Who is in your circle, and do they help you become better?

---

## Tuesday: Patronage and Stewardship

### Morning: Memory Work (5 min)

Memorize:

> Use your gifts to help good things grow.

Older students may also copy Lorenzo's line:

> Chi vuol esser lieto, sia: di doman non c'è certezza.

Discuss how joy can be either gratitude or forgetfulness.

---

### Afternoon: Build a Florence Shelf (30-40 min)

Create a small display that honors learning and beauty.

Include:
- One excellent book
- One beautiful image or drawing
- One natural object
- One handwritten quotation
- One tool for making or learning (pencil, ruler, brush, instrument, etc.)

**Ages 8-10:** Name the shelf and explain each object.  
**Ages 11-14:** Add a paragraph: "What this shelf teaches me to love."  
**Ages 15-18:** Add a design note: "How environment shapes attention."

---

## Wednesday: Crisis and Courage

### Morning: The Pazzi Conspiracy (20-30 min)

Tell the story at the right level:

**Ages 8-10:** Enemies attacked Lorenzo and his brother; Lorenzo survived and worked to protect the city.  
**Ages 11-14:** Explain the cathedral attack, Giuliano's death, the wider political crisis, and Lorenzo's journey to Naples.  
**Ages 15-18:** Discuss faction, papal politics, retaliation, and how crises can consolidate power.

**Discussion questions:**
- What made Lorenzo's journey to Naples brave?
- Can a crisis make a leader both more necessary and more dangerous?
- What should a leader do after surviving an attack: seek revenge, restore peace, or both?

---

### Afternoon: Courage Map (20 min)

Make three columns:

1. Danger Lorenzo faced
2. Choice he made
3. What virtue or risk appeared

Older students should include at least one risk of power consolidation after the crisis.

---

## Thursday: The Warning

### Morning: Debate (30 min)

Topic:

> Lorenzo was good for Florence.

Assign or choose sides.

**Arguments for:**
- Supported art, learning, and humanism
- Helped save Florence after the Pazzi crisis
- Made Florence culturally influential

**Arguments against:**
- Concentrated power in one family
- Weakened republican habits
- Neglected financial foundations
- Built a system too dependent on himself

After debate, each student writes:

> Lorenzo was admirable because ___. Lorenzo was dangerous because ___.

---

### Afternoon: Institution Test (20-30 min)

Ask of any group the student knows:

- Does this group depend too much on one person?
- What happens if that person leaves?
- Are younger members being trained?
- Are the rules and habits clear?

**Ages 8-10:** Discuss with examples from family chores or teams.  
**Ages 11-14:** Write a half-page.  
**Ages 15-18:** Write a one-page institutional durability audit.

---

## Friday: Application and Synthesis

### Morning: Patronage Plan (30 min)

Design a plan to help one good thing grow.

**Ages 8-10:** Choose one person or project to encourage this week.  
**Ages 11-14:** Design a small workshop, reading group, art corner, or family project.  
**Ages 15-18:** Create a $10,000 community patronage plan with safeguards against ego and dependency.

Include:
- What you want to help
- What resources it needs
- Who should be involved
- How it will continue after your first help

---

### Afternoon: Final Discussion (20-30 min)

Ask:

1. What did Lorenzo use well?
2. What did Lorenzo risk or misuse?
3. What is the difference between generosity and control?
4. How can beauty lead people toward virtue?
5. What gift or influence do you have right now?

Close by repeating:

> Use your gifts to help good things grow.

---

## Optional Extensions

### Art History
Look at a Botticelli painting or an early Michelangelo sculpture. Ask: What conditions made this work possible?

### Civics
Compare Florence's informal Medici rule with a modern organization where one family, donor, founder, or platform has outsized influence.

### Writing
Older students write: "Will this still work when the founder is gone?" using Lorenzo's Florence as the opening example.
